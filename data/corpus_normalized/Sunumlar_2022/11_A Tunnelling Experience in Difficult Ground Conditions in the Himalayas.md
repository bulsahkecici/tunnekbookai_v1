## An invitation received from NHPC  (National Hydroelectric Power Corporation) India

<!-- image -->

On the 14.07 2007 Narendra Kumar Executive Director (D&amp;E) Design &amp; Engineering wrote:

NHPC Ltd.NHPC is in the process of constituting a Panel of Experts(POE) for resolving the Geo-technical and engineering difficulties being faced in one of the tunnels of a hydro power project namely Parbati-II and is desirous of utilizing your expertise in the field of tunneling in complex/adverse geological conditions as a member in a Panel of Experts (POE). NHPC has gone through critical geological conditions during excavation of HRT through TBM due to hard and abrasive nature of Manikaran Quartzite and other stressinduced instabilities.

Parbati HE Project, Stage-II, located in Kullu District of Himachal Pradesh in India, is under construction by NHPC as a run of the river scheme on river Parbati.  The project mainly comprises construction of a 84m high Concrete Gravity Dam across river Parbati, 31.52 km long, 6.0m diameter Horse Shoe/circular shaped Head Race Tunnel, 3.5m diameter, 2 Nos. inclined Pressure Shafts and a Surface Power House. The Power House utilizes a gross head of 862.5m and shall have installed capacity of 800 MW (4 x 200 MW).

## TBM, Its Performance,  Geology and associated Problems

<!-- image -->

- Atlas Copco, MK 27 Jarva make of 1992, reconditioned full-face hard rock, powerful, open style Main Beam TBM has been deployed for excavation.
- Out of 31.52 km long HRT, approx. 8.5 km length of HRT was to be excavated through TBM. At present 4.8 km length of HRT has been excavated through TBM and about 3.7 km is yet to be excavated. Out of 4.8km length of HRT excavated till date through TBM, 3.47km length of tunneling has been negotiated through Bandal Granite and 1.33km has been negotiated through Manikaran Quartzite. From overall experience of tunneling in Manikaran Quartzite, it was found that the progress of boring in this rock by TBM is far from satisfactory. One of the reasons for the same is abrasivity of quartzite vis-a-vis cutter efficiency leading to poor penetration rate.  It is pertinent to mention here that during tunneling by TBM, even during the earlier phase, the average cutter consumption in quartzite was 0.88m/cutter whereas in case of Granitic gneiss the same was 4.85m/cutter, indicating higher cutter consumption in quartzite.

## Following Geological problems have been encountered during TBM excavation:

<!-- image -->

- During excavation of HRT, few weak/shear zones have been encountered in Manikaran Quartzite along with substantial ingress of groundwater (amounting to as high as 7000l/min encountered in one of the probe holes) hampering progress of excavation. The shear/ weak zones have been overcome by adopting suitable treatment measures in the form of forepoling, chemical grouting, pre grouting, erection of McNally plates, steel ribs with concrete backfill etc. Specialized treatment methodologies for tackling EGO (Extraordinary Geological Occurrences) conditions like construction of intersection chamber and side tunnel, drilling of long geognostic holes for interpreting the extent of shear zones ahead of face, carrying out chemical and consolidation grouting from side tunnel, sidra drains for drainage of groundwater, BOP (Blow out preventer) to control excessive flow of silt along with water have also been adopted in the past.
- Apart from above major problems, other stress induced incidences like moderate rock bursting have also been experienced in hard &amp; brittle Manikaran Quartzite due to presence of high superincumbent cover ranging from approx. 900-1600M above tunnel grade, leading to heavy overbreak.

## Suggested Scope of PoE

The panel of experts shall visit the site as and when required and suggest suitable measures to deal with the site conditions. The panel of experts to be engaged shall give suggestions on the followings:

- (i) Evaluation of TBM comprising of operation and maintenance and modifications if any for achieving desired progress, working of TBM, deriving maximum output on given site condition and EGO treatment etc. to achieve proper/required progress.
- (ii) Evaluation of tunneling conditions and suggestion of suitable treatment methodologies for tackling EGO conditions.

The period of assignment will be initially for Six months w.e.f. Sep, 2017 to Feb, 2018. The frequency of visit by experts shall be as per requirement but could be for a period of one week in every quarter or as and when required.

- Boring diameter : 6.80m

## LIMITATIONS OF TBM

- The Probe drill system mounted on the TBM can practically drill from 10:30 o'clock to 1:30 o'clock positions. Similarly, the rockbolters can also drill in the upper part of crown only.
- By virtue of the age of TBM (more than 25 years old), although the machine remains powerful, yet its operation and maintenance is a difficult task.
- It is difficult to arrange specific spares as many of the components have become obsolete.
- The operation of such TBM's in todays' time is also not an easy task as most of the workforce is not acquainted in working on such old TBM's.
- Yet, these obstacles &amp; inherent limitations have been overcome to some extent and best possible measures to maintain the TBM in good working condition, troubleshoot the problems and achieving an optimal rate of progress, are being tried.

## Expected Daily Advance Rate

- The expected daily advance rate may be calculated from the Following Data of boring on the 5th of October 2017
- 'Mean net advance rate 20.2 mm/min
- Mean rpm 3.92 rev/min
- Penetration = 20.2(mm/min)/3.92(rev/min)
- Penetration =5mm/rev'
- Daily advance rate formulae = penetration (mm/rev) x revolution speed (rev/min) x daily working time x machine utilization time
- rate =
- Daily advance 5(mm/rev)x3.92(rev/min)x24x60(min)x0.15
- i.e. equal to: 4.2 m/day
- Even with 15% of machine utilization time, daily advance rate is expected to be 4.2 m/day

## The possibility of Using Thrust Index to Predict Weak Zones and Some Comments on Probe Drill Penetration Rates.

<!-- image -->

- Probe drilling is applied systematically in Parbati II Project and the penetration rates (PR) the color of the return water and rock cutting are used to detect weak zones. The values between 85-100 cm/min are accepted fast penetration rates and the values greater than 100 cm/min are accepted very fast penetration rates. Further actions are taken based upon these values.
- A weak schist band was observed during November 2017 and shear zone with heavy water and silt was experienced in February 2018 resulting several weeks of delay. As indicated in PoE report II, December was one the best month in that respect. The probe drill data of these three months were analyzed to see the accuracy and reliability of probe drilling on detecting weak zones and the frequency of changing of penetration rates .
- As emphasized in the 2 nd PoE report a thrust index was defined as an indicator of predicting weak zones and below the value of 1000 kN/(mm/rev) the probability of crossing with weak zones were high. To check the validity of this concept the variation of the thrust index values with number of strokes of TBM from 14 th May to 13 th June was plotted . This Figure shows that passing from competent rock (26 th May-39 th stroke) to weak rock (30 th May-36 th stroke) is gradual. In that weak zone 2200kg of micro fine cement was used. Again the same trend was observed between 66 th stroke to 74 th stroke and a schist band was encountered on the 12 th of June. In this area probe drill penetration rates were determined as fast and very fast

## What we Learned From Parbatti II Project ?

- Newer use an old TBM in difficult ground conditions
- The best choise in fractured rock with low RQD TBM is to use a shielded machine and precast segments
- Probe drilling does'nt mean just drilling ahead of a tunnel face, apart from the geology, drilling Thrust and rpm of drill affect drilling rate olso, so use a drilling data acqusiton system to be sure from interpretation of drilling rate.
- Double component polimers are the best choice for excess water ingress and binding the loose rock.
