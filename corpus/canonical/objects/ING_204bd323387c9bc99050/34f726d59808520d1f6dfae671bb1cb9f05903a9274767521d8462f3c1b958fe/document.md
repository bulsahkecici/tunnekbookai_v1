### ArcelorMittal are expanding the boundaries of SFRC in large diameter TBM tunnels

Steel fibre reinforced concrete (SFRC) is now widely accepted as a sole reinforcement solution in small-medium diameter tunnels. However, its adoption in large diameter TBM tunnels has, to date, remained limited.

This caution reflects several factors, including the higher structural demands of larger spans, stringent crack control and durability requirements, and the inherent conservatism embedded within current design codes. Perceived risk at the design and planning stage also contributes. Yet, these concerns sit alongside a clear and growing driver within the industry: the need to improve productivity, reduce costs, enhance safety in handling and installation, and deliver more sustainable construction solutions - all of which align strongly with the advantages of SFRC.

The Świnoujście road tunnel in Poland provides a compelling example of how these challenges can be successfully addressed. Constructed beneath the Świna River, the 1.48 km tunnel presented highly variable geological conditions, with the most demanding section beneath the Świna Strait. Here, the blow-up pressure was low due to the low overburden (minimum 10m), while the stability pressure was high due to the depth. These contrasting conditions demanded both careful TBM operation and a highly optimised segment design.

Despite this complexity, excavation using a 13.46m diameter slurry TBM was completed without major settlement issues. The tunnel’s 12m internal diameter makes it the largest in Europe to date to rely solely on steel fibre reinforcement.

The 500mm segmental lining was reinforced using ArcelorMittal HE++ 90/60 steel fibres at a dosage of 43kg/m³, achieving the targeted Model Code 5d performance class (fR1k > 5.0 MPa; fR3k > 5.5 MPa), with initial testing exceeding requirements.

This project demonstrates that ultra-high-performance steel fibres can meet the demands of large-diameter tunnelling while enabling gains in efficiency, safety, and sustainability - opening the door to even larger applications in the near future.
