## ABSTRACT

Constructing underground tunnels and associated infrastructure in tectonically sensitive mountainous regions such as Bilaspur, HimachalPradesh,presentssignificant engineering challenges due to variable rock mass properties, complex deformational behavior and weak litho-structural conditions. Detailed pre-investigation of geological and structural characteristics is therefore essential to evaluate rock stability prior to infrastructure development, particularly in steep terrains that exhibit elevated geotechnical and environmental risks. This study assesses rock mass quality using the rock mass rating (RMR) geomechanical classification system based on detailed field investigations, including intact rock strength, rock quality designation (RQD), discontinuity spacing, discontinuity condition, groundwater condition, and discontinuity orientation. To enhance the reliability of stability assessment, geographic information system (GIS)-based spatial analysis was integrated to evaluate terrain slope, fault distribution, lineaments and regional seismicity. Although GIS parameters were not used directly in RMR computation, they were employed as an independent spatial validation framework to examine the consistency between geomechanical classification and regional structural controls. The results indicate RMR values ranging from 21 to 30, corresponding to Class IV (poor rock mass quality), primarily controlled by closely spaced discontinuities and unfavorable structural orientations. The novelty of this study lies in integrating field-based geomechanical classification with GIS-derived tectonic and geomorphological analyses to provide spatial validation of tunnel stability conditions in a tectonically sensitive Himalayan terrain, enabling improved assessment of engineering feasibility beyond conventional RMR evaluation. The findings suggest that tunnel construction in the study area requires enhanced support systems, including the New Austrian Tunneling Method (NATM), rock bolting, steel ribs, and shotcrete to ensure long-term stability. This integrated framework provides a practical basis for planning railway tunnel infrastructure in structurally complex mountain environments.

Rock mass rating (RMR) is the rock classification system widely used to categorize the rocks based on their stability, their deformational behaviors and its technical application. The system evaluates the rock mass stability by analyzing various parameters which includes uniaxial compressive strength, RQD, Strength of intact rock, Spacing of discontinuity, Drill core quality RQD (%), Weathering and Groundwater conditions. Intrinsic properties of rock mass at a particular site are classified based on their geological history and surroundings [10]. The safety of the structure is influenced by factors like ground condition, in-situ stress and excavation methods; this uncertainty can be addressed by the detailed investigation of the parameters associated with the local rock mass behavior and their surrounding regions. To ensure a strong and safe construction, the RMR Classification was incorporated in our study to evaluate the stability of the rock and to determine the required support class methods. A Rock Mass classification known as geomechanics classification or the RMR system. It can be found mainly by the collection of fields data [11, 12]. Evaluation of the RMR parameters of an area provides the stability value of the region. The RMR calculation system evaluates the stability of the region and categorizes it within the scale of I-V in the decreasing order of rock classes.

Based on this resultant Statical value and rock class, the rock mass strength and properties of the study area is categorized and if the result suggests the stability of the region lies poor or very poor category the instillation of additional Supporting predefined construction System or support system is required along with normal construction methods to ensure the safety and stability of the tunnel during the excavations.

Different types of tunnel construction methods or support systems are opted to construct a tunnel, but it is purely depending upon the geographic and stability condition. Analyzing factors like the stability of terrain, geology, seismic activity of the area, the engineers will opt for the suitable tunnel construction method. Shield Tunneling, Bored Tunneling, Cut and Cover Method, horizontal directional drilling (HDD), New Austrian Tunneling Method (NATM) are most popular among the other tunnel construction methods. NATM is a method that integrates rock mass assessment with the use of controlled excavation and timely support installation to maintain tunnel stability. India is a very vast country, and different regions exhibit different geological and geographical features; different methods are chosen to find the stability of the particular rock or whole region [13-15]. The study might vary from place to place according to the characteristics of the environment.

NATM is not a construction method for tunneling; it's a strategy for the smooth work of all the construction activities with safety and economy being the main principles [15]. A method of producing underground space by using all available means to develop the maximum self-supporting capacity of the rock or soil itself to provide the stability of the underground opening [16, 17]. Since the development of the NATM in the 1960s, this technology has been effectively employed in several tunnels. However, opinions about NATM principles emerged until 2000, claiming that NATM is not a tunneling method but rather an approach that incorporates all generic tunneling principles. NATM generally acts as a self-support class to the structure and ensures its longer stability over different time periods. The NATM is based on the principle of maximizing the capacity of the ground to sustain its own weight by precisely and rationally balancing all the pressures that affect the surrounding rock [18-20]. The NATM method mainly involves two major types of support class. The first system involves a flexible outer arch or protective support designed to balance the structure. The system is reinforced by additional steel ribs, bolts, and shotcrete [21]. The second support system involves an inner concrete lining. However, this lining is not fabricated until the outer lining reaches the balance [22].

This research addresses the challenges in constructing underground railway tunnel by adopting combined framework of rock mass classifications (Figure 1) and GIS based analysis in the study area which provides a basic understanding of the rock mass behavior and this sensitivity analysis offers a systemic approach toward the construction activity in the Himalayan region, exhibiting a complex geological condition. The existing studies have carried out their work on the application of the rock mass classification (RMR) and the other geotechnical classification systems to find the stability of the rock for designing tunnel construction in Northern India. But the widespread use of the RMR methods needs additional validation and reliability for the prediction of tunnel stability, especially in a region like Bilaspur, which is considered a high-risk zone due to its complex structure. To address this, along with the geotechnical classification systems, the incorporation of GIS-based spatial study helps to analyze the local geological features like faults, seismicity, slope, and lineaments which strongly control the tectonic activity in these regions. Proper and systematic data collection prior to tunnel construction provides an initial understanding of regional stability, while during excavation, geotechnical investigations combined with GIS-based spatial analyses help validate drilling techniques and support class methods, such as those adopted in the NATM approach. Existing works have inadequately applied this methodology in high-risk zones like Bilaspur. This GIS-RMR integrated methodology performs as an additional layer of validation; the areas or regions classified as weaker sections based on the RMR rating correlate well with the findings of zones of increased fault activity and seismic influence identified through GIS analysis, providing additional support for identification of rock mass weakness along the region and opting for suitable excavation methods. Based on this provided information by this study, further systematic reliable data collection would provide the integration of machine-learning and 3D geological modeling methods within the GIS-RMR framework, providing an improved and accurate prediction of rock mass stability over the study area.

## 2.2 | Geological Settings

The Himachal Pradesh has one of the oldest geology histories from the Pre-Cambrian period [26]. The geological formation range in age from the Paleoproterozoic to the Present and is primarily composed of sedimentary and igneous origins. The Himalayan geology is majorly categorized into five major regions: South Tibetan Detachment (STD), Siwalik Region, Lesser Himalayan Region, Indo Gangetic Plain, Tons Thrust System, and Hanging-Wall Structures [27]. They were created when the Indian Carton collided with each other around 50-60 million years ago. The regional geology is divided into Sub-Groups, major formations including the Siwalik Hills, Sutlej Intermountain Valley, Sirowal Formations, and Alluvial Plains. The drainage basin of the river region makes up the most remote portion of hill ranges in the western Himalayan foothill belt, where river formations are predominant in the outermost Sub-Himalayan Cenozoic Belt as a result of the Himalaya's climb over the Cenozoic period. Major Frontal Thrust or the Lesser Himalayan Proterozoic series is overlain by this belt, which rises abruptly above the Indo-Gangetic alluvial plains along the Main Boundary Thrust [28]. The oldest sequence is the Eocene marine transgression Subathu Formation, followed by the fluvial Dagshai, Kasauli, and Siwalik Groups. The Proterozoic lesser Himalayan sedimentary Zone includes the low-lying hills to the north of the MBT and the southern portions of the Main Central Thrust. The major principal zones are Higher Himalayan Crystalline (HHC), Tethyan Sedimentary Zone (TSZ), and Tso-Morali crystalline. Although the Bilaspur forms part of the broader Himalayan tectonic framework, the rock mass conditions throughout the study area are controlled primarily by the Siwalik Formations (Figure 3). The tunnel excavation sites are predominated by sandstones, siltstones, and mudstones with folding development of joints and faulting due to the local tectonic activity [29, 30]. Claystone and sandstone from the Lower Siwalik have been exposed in the south-western section that is severely sheared and fractured, possibly as a result of a thrust or fault activity [31]. Rocks from the Middle Siwalik have predominated the section that is mostly composed of light gray fine to coarse grained, soft to compact sandstone, and red clays. The Upper Siwalik group comprises earthy, light gray, friable, micaceous sandstone, and sand rock interbedded with maroon, brown, and green claystone in the lower part [32]. Pink colored clay and siltstone with lenticular bands of pebbly sand rock are found in the upper part of this unit.

Slope maps are indicator in land use planning to identify regions of sever steep terrain which may unsuitable for construction activity and places prone to frequent landslides or erosion leads to significant challenges during excavations. Slope analysis is favored to locates mild sloped regions that could be appropriate for agriculture, grazing, or other land uses. Slope maps act as a tool in identifying the regions of significant erosion risk or possible water runoff and helps in decisions making regarding land management and conservation initiatives [36, 37]. And, to estimate the danger of natural hazards such as landslides, rockfalls and avalanches [38]. ## 2.5 | Lineament Map of the Study Area

The lineaments are considered one of the major parameters that need to be analyzed during the Pre-excavation activity of tunnel construction, as it directly connected with faults and intersect with zones of weakness. On the surface of the Earth, lineaments are linear characteristics that frequently indicate underlying geological formations including shear zones, faults, and fractures. Fractured zones associated with lineaments act as conduits for groundwater flow, which affects the stability of the construction and collapses the structure. DEM data to generate the Lineament Map was downloaded from the Bhuvan portal and processed in ArcGIS. Hillshade layers were generated using different azimuth and elevation angles, active lineaments were identified and digitized from the multi-azimuth hill shade, and the extracted lineaments were subsequently used to generate a lineament density map of Bilaspur using the line density tool in ArcGIS for thrust and structural investigations. The appearance of dense lineaments indicates the region has highly fractured or jointed rock and may require reinforcement techniques like rock bolting, shotcreting, or grouting to improve the stability. ## 2.8.1 | Strength of Intact Rock Material

The strength of intact rock in the Bilaspur was assessed to recognize the ability of the Siwalik sandstone and mudstone units to resist failure during excavation [50]. The Seismic Spatial map key finding was the clustering of seismic activity in specific parts of the study area. ## 4 | Discussion

Upon performing the RMR, the results indicate that the Bilaspur tunnel alignment predominantly falls within Class IV of Poor rock mass category. Rock masses in this category are generally unstable and cannot safely support excavation without the implementation of systematic support measures. The major RMR parameters includes Spacing of Discontinuity evaluate the distance between two separated rocks, if joints show no discontinuity or very minimal the structure is identified as good, by identifying them it will be easier to plan the tunnel excavation

[54-56]. And other parameters we need to look into is groundwater, if inflow is excessive, it can disrupt construction activities and, in severe cases, cause collateral damage or even lead to tunnel collapse. Our Study Region lies in the elevated region and slopes of the Bilaspur, as we are using the 'tunnelling in rock masses' for the construction of the tunnel. ## 5 | Conclusion and Recommendations

The Rock Mass Properties and the structural stability of the study area Bilaspur, Himachal Pradesh has been identified using the RMR89 Support class system and integrating GIS spatial distribution maps to identify the rock patterns, variation in the lithology and structural condition. The study area lies in the upper Siwalik of Bilaspur, Himachal Pradesh which is influenced by steep slopes, closed spaced joints and frequent seismic activity; this unstable geomorphic condition leads to the poor RMR rating of Class IV and intimates that the rock present in the region is quite weak to hold the tunnel excavation for a longer duration. To ensure the tunnel's safety and stability, the study recommends the use of an additional support system, especially NATM including bolting, steel ribs or lattice girders, and thick shotcrete to ensure structural stability during excavation. The implementation of the NATM approach for tunnel construction in Bilaspur upper Siwalik assures the tunnel stability and security, even in minor seismically active times. The proper scientific analysis of rock structures and rock mass properties guarantees the safety and long-term stability of infrastructure developments in the upper Siwalik region of Bilaspur. The integrated RMR along with the GIS geological approach identifies the key factors controlling stability and rock mass properties; this approach is essential for recognizing the geotechnical characteristics of sedimentary formations and assessing their appropriateness for tunnel excavations.
