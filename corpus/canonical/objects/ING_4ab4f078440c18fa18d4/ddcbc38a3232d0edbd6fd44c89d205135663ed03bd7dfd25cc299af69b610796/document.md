## ABSTRACT

In 2017, a 1.7-mile-long tunnel was dug using the largest tunnel-boring machine in the world. As construction workers seek to complete the new double-decker roadway tunnel by the end of 2018, the 7,000-ton, 326-footlong machine completes its task and is disassembled.

## I. INTRODUCTION

A tunnel boring machine created especially for the Seattle, Washington, United States, and Alaskan Way Viaduct Replacement  Tunnel  Project  by  the  Washington  State  Department  of  Transportation  (WSDOT).  [1]  It  was created by the Japanese company Hitachi Zosen Sakai Works, and it was assembled in Seattle in June 2013. The machine was set to start tunnel boring on July 30, 2013, and finish it in December 2015. A sudden obstruction forced the work to stop on December 6, 2013, around 1,083 feet (330 m) into the route's 9,270-foot (2,830 m) planned length. In 2002, near the Alaskan Way Viaduct, a steel pipe that had been used to detect groundwater was thought to have struck numerous cutting blades, causing damage. The primary bearing seal system had some damage,  though,  which  contributed  to  the  bearing  overheating  during  operation,  according  to  a  later examination.  In order to reach and remove the machine's cutter head for maintenance and partial replacement in 2015, a recovery trench was excavated from the surface during the course of the following two years.

## II. DESIGN AND ASSEMBLY

With a cutter head diameter of 57.5 feet (17.5 m), Bertha is the largest earth pressure balance tunnel boring equipment in the world and was created by Hitachi Zosen Sakai Works in Osaka, Japan. The machine weighed 6,700 short tons and was 326 feet (99 meters) in length (6,100 t). The project's contractors, Seattle  Tunnel Partners,  purchased the equipment for $80 million.  Seattle Tunnel Partners is a  partnership between Tutor Perini Company, based in Sylmar, California, and Dragados USA, a wholly owned subsidiary of Dragados, S.A., the construction arm of the Spanish ACS Group.

On December 20, 2012, Hitachi Zosen performed a naming ceremony and a completion ceremony for the device in Osaka, Japan. In February 2013, repairs were needed after test assembly and shakedown on Bertha in Japan

## IV. CONCLUSION

A  tunnel  boring  machine  (TBM)  is  the  machine  used to  excavate  tunnels.  TBMs  can  drill  through  a  range  of ground surfaces, including sand and hard rock. Tunnel boring was completed on April 4, 2017, and the finished tunnel opened to traffic on February 4, 2019. Practically none of Bertha's components were reusable, and most of its steel was melted and recycled. The final, disassembled pieces of Bertha were removed from the tunnel portal in August 2017.
