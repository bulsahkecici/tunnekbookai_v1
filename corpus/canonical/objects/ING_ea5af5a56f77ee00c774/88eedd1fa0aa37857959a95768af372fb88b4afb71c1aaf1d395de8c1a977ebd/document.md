## Damage Categories And Inspection Techniques For Single–Shell Segmental Lined Tunnels During Their Operational Phase

### Damage Categories And Inspection Techniques For Single–Shell Segmental Lined Tunnels During Their Operational Phase

Activity Group 2 of WG 06 of the ITA-AITES was formed in 2021 and deals with topics related to preventive maintenance and tunnel refurbishment. In many countries, different tunnelling techniques have been used in the past to construct tunnels, leading to specific problems that need to be solved with specific refurbishment and maintenance activities. In all member nations represented in the working group, segmental bored tunnel linings are present. Therefore, this was a logical first topic to investigate to share knowledge on the specific damage originating during the service lifetime of the tunnel. The resulting report compiles many examples of both older and more recently constructed bored tunnels that have suffered damage and needed repair or refurbishment during the operational phase, whether after several years or decades after initial construction. This report has been written keeping the tunnel owner in mind, but can be useful to all persons active in the inspection, monitoring and or maintenance of bored tunnels. ITA WG 02 has previously published a guideline on damage in bored tunnelling, where the focus was mainly on the construction phase of the project, with the aim of preventing damage from occurring during production, transport and construction of the segmental lining. One of the main recommendations is to make sure that all damages (and its eventual repair) is registered at the end of the construction phase to make sure that it can be followed up during the operational phase of the tunnel. The behaviour of a segmental tunnel is subject to change over time. Therefore, the main focus of this damage catalogue is the operational phase of the tunnel. It is important to distinguish ‘stable’ damage stemming from the construction phase from ‘developing’ damage initiated or evolving during the service life. Especially because damage initiated during the operational phase can be progressive over time and can ultimately lead to failure. The mechanism behind this damage is often not foreseen in the design of the tunnel or is due to changing circumstances in the surrounding of the tunnel. Also, defects originating from the construction phase may be a source for further deterioration. It should be unnecessary to say that inspection and monitoring over time are essential measures to mitigate further damage and ultimate failure. Chapter 1 of this report briefly describes the construction method of bored tunnelling and describes the ways that damage is induced, as well as the importance of distinguishing the phase in which it occurs. In Chapter 2, different monitoring techniques are described. Inspection and monitoring techniques are based on several physical measurement principles which do not change. But the techniques used in the field do change rapidly with regard to resolution, data acquisition, data storage and processing. Inspection by persons knowing the design and history of the tunnel, with expertise on specific damage types will always be necessary once damage is detected with these techniques, but at the same time the developments in automated measurement techniques and automatic intelligence to process the data are promising methods to facilitate and support preventive maintenance. Finally, in Chapter 3, the different damage types and the consequences they may have for the tunnel structure as well as the tunnel users are presented in detail, along with the typical ways to monitor them.
