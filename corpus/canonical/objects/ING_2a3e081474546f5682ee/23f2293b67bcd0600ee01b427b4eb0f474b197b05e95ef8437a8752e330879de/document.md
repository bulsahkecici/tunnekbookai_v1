### Description

Tunnel construction is one of the most complex and significant engineering activities in modern infrastructure development. Tunnels play a crucial role in transportation, water supply, hydropower generation, mining, urban transit systems, and underground utility networks. Rapid urbanization, increasing transportation demand, mountainous terrain development, and expansion of metro rail systems have accelerated tunnel construction activities worldwide. However, tunnel engineering involves numerous technical, geological, environmental, and safety challenges due to underground excavation conditions and complex subsurface behavior. This article presents a comprehensive review of tunnel construction methods and associated challenges, including conventional and modern tunneling techniques, geological investigations, excavation systems, support structures, ventilation systems, safety measures, and environmental considerations. The study examines methods such as the cut-and-cover method, drill-and-blast technique, New Austrian Tunneling Method (NATM), Tunnel Boring Machines (TBM), immersed tunnels, and microtunneling systems. The paper also discusses challenges related to geological uncertainties, groundwater intrusion, structural stability, ventilation, worker safety, cost escalation, and environmental impacts. Modern technologies such as GIS, remote sensing, Building Information Modeling (BIM), artificial intelligence, and smart monitoring systems used in tunnel construction are also analyzed. The findings indicate that successful tunnel construction requires advanced engineering planning, geotechnical analysis, risk management, and sustainable construction practices to ensure structural safety and long-term operational efficiency.
