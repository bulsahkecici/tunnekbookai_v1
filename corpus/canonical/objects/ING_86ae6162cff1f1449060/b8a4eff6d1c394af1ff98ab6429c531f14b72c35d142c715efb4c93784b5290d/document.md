## 1. Introduction

In recent years, there has been a significant increase in the use of earth pressure balance-tunnel boring machines (EPB-TBM) in urban areas, largely due to the machine ' s adaptability to various geological conditions. The EPB-TBM is a specialized type of TBM designed for excavating tunnels in soft ground conditions where there is a high risk of ground settlement or water ingress. The methodology of an EPB-TBM involves a unique process that allows for safe and efficient tunnel excavation in challenging geological conditions [1].

The EPB-TBM operates by controlling the pressure within the excavation chamber to balance the earth and water pressure against the tunnel face. This balance helps to prevent ground settlement and maintain the stability of the tunnel during excavation. The methodology of an EPB TBM typically involves the following key steps [2 - 5].

## A B S T R A C T

The analysis of cutterhead torque and thrust force is of utmost importance in the selection of a proper tunnel boring machine (TBM) and in achieving optimal operation of the earth pressure balance (EPB) machine for a specific project. This study focuses on the analysis of cutterhead torque and thrust force using data collected from the recent metro project of Tehran, specifically the southern extension of Tehran metro line 6, (TML6-SE). To assess the impact of geotechnical and operational parameters on the measured torque and thrust, a database consisting of 457 data points was established. The excavation of a significant portion of the project took place in a dense and  robust  limestone  using  a  refurbished  EPB  machine  with  a  diameter  of  9.19  m,  initially designed for soft ground excavation. To complete this section of the tunnel, the cutterhead was equipped  with  disc  cutters  instead  of  rippers.  Consequently,  existing  theoretical  models  for estimating torque and thrust of an EPB machine were modified to accommodate the hard rock boring process. Additionally, the Takagi-Sugeno fuzzy system (TSFS) was utilized in this study. The  results  of  the  models  yielded  quantitative  conclusions,  and  the  comparison  between  the measured and predicted target parameters demonstrated high accuracy. This study contributes to the understanding of the influence of various parameters on the performance of EPB machines in hard rock boring conditions, as well as providing insight into the potential adjustments needed in theoretical  models  for  predicting  cutterhead  torque  and  thrust  force.  The  proposed  models showed satisfactory and reliable accuracy, with all models having an R2 value above 0.7 and loss function values nearing zero.

- Excavation: The EPB TBM uses a rotating cutterhead equipped with cutting tools to excavate the tunnel face. As the cutterhead rotates, it breaks up the soil or soft rock, which is then transported to the rear of the machine (back removal system) for removal [2].
- Pressure Control: One of the critical aspects of the EPB TBM methodology is maintaining the pressure within the excavation chamber to balance the external earth and water pressure. This pressure control is achieved by adjusting the screw conveyor speed, the rate of soil conditioning injection, and the operation of the segment erector [3].
- Soil Conditioning: To facilitate excavation and maintain pressure balance, a slurry or foam-based soil conditioning agent is injected into the excavation chamber. This agent helps to stabilize the excavated material, reduce friction, and control the pressure within the chamber.
- Segmental Lining: As the EPB TBM advances, precast concrete segments are installed to form the tunnel lining. The segment erector within the machine positions and places these segments in the tunnel, providing immediate support and stability to the excavated tunnel [4].
- Monitoring and Control: Throughout the excavation process, engineers closely monitor and control various parameters such as cutterhead torque, thrust force, pressure levels, and ground settlement. This real-time monitoring allows for adjustments to be made to ensure the safe and efficient progress of the tunneling operation.

Overall, the methodology of an EPB TBM involves a combination of excavation, pressure control, soil conditioning, segmental lining installation, and meticulous monitoring to ensure the successful and safe construction of tunnels in soft ground conditions. By following this methodology, EPB TBMs can effectively navigate through challenging geological formations while minimizing the risk of ground settlement and maintaining tunnel stability.

Analyzing cutterhead torque and thrust force in TBM and EPB machines is crucial for various reasons. Initially, cutterhead torque directly impacts the power needed to excavate the tunnel face. Understanding the torque levels required for different geological conditions allows engineers to choose the appropriate machine with the necessary power capacity to efficiently cut through rock or soil. This ensures that the TBM or EPB machine can excavate the tunnel effectively without excessive wear and tear or potential breakdowns due to insufficient power [2,3,6].

Secondly, thrust force plays a vital role in the progress of the TBM or EPB machine through the ground. The thrust force generated by the machine ' s thrust jack mechanisms is crucial for pushing the cutterhead forward and maintaining tunneling progress. Analyzing thrust force helps determine the optimal operating parameters to prevent machine jamming during excavation [6].

Furthermore,  analyzing  cutterhead  torque  and  thrust  force  is  essential  for  selecting  the  most  suitable  machine  for  specific geological conditions. Different types of TBMs and EPB machines are designed to handle varying levels of torque and thrust force based on factors like rock hardness, soil composition, and groundwater conditions. By evaluating these parameters, engineers can choose the right machine that can navigate through the ground effectively without facing excessive resistance or operational challenges.

Overall, a comprehensive analysis of cutterhead torque and thrust force is vital for optimizing TBM and EPB machine selection and operation. By understanding and monitoring these critical parameters, engineers can improve the efficiency, productivity, utilization factor, and safety of tunneling projects, ultimately leading to successful and cost-effective tunnel construction.

Fundamentally, EPB machines are designed for excavating in soft ground and soil environments, with their mechanical and physical characteristics tailored to suit these conditions. As a result, their use in rock conditions is not typical. However, with tech­ nological advancements and the increasing complexity of geological conditions, tunneling machine manufacturing companies have begun creating boring machines capable of excavating in mixed-face environments, accommodating the geological engineering and geotechnical intricacies of the area.

In the specific case of the line 6 metro of Tehran, southern extension, which traverses urban and soil environments, EPB machines were employed for construction. It is important to note that a portion of this metro line intersects a carbonate rock unit, necessitating the adaptation of the excavation machine. In these instances, disc cutters are installed on the cutterhead to replace the standard cutting tools used for soil drilling, enabling efficient drilling in the rock section.

Challenges arise when attempting to predict the performance of the boring machine in such specialized conditions. The empirical and analytical relationships typically utilized for such predictions are rendered ineffective due to the varying geological conditions and physical properties of the cutterhead. Consequently, existing models lack the required precision for accurate predictions in these circumstances, highlighting the need for a specialized model tailored to these specific conditions.

As a response to this need for innovation, this article aims to refine existing models under normal conditions to better align with the unique conditions of this project. By adjusting the mathematical structure and input parameters of these models, the goal is to enhance the accuracy of the results obtained. These refined models can then be applied to similar projects facing comparable geological, geotechnical, and physical challenges, offering a more precise and tailored approach to tunneling in complex geological environments.

## 2. Literature survey

The EPB machine offers high efficiency and minimal ground disturbance, making it a favorable choice for urban construction projects, as it ensures optimal financial and planning outcomes [4,6]. While EPB-TBMs are commonly used in soft ground, their application in rock formations requires special considerations. Evaluation of operating parameters, such as cutterhead torque and thrust force, is crucial for controlling project schedules and budgets when using EPB machines. These parameters are closely related to the geotechnical properties of materials at the tunnel face [6 - 8]. Cutterhead torque and thrust force are vital operating parameters, with cutterhead torque directly linked to driving power [7]. Accurate evaluation of operational parameters is essential during the design phase of EPB-TBM for terrains with specific geological conditions. EPB-TBMs are responsible for tunnel face excavation and supporting chamber pressure to stabilize face pressure [9]. In recent decades, researchers have conducted thorough investigations into the EPB operating system, particularly focusing on cutterhead torque and thrust force using theoretical and mathematical methods based on laboratory studies and physical modeling [6,10,11]. These studies have highlighted the strong influence of geotechnical properties of the ground and machine specifications on operational parameters [6,10 - 12].

Shi et al. (2011) introduced mathematical expressions derived from a physical model and theoretical calculations to estimate the cutterhead torque of the EPBM. This included an analytical method to calculate the overall torque of the cutterhead by examining the interaction between machine characteristics factors and the strength parameters of soil materials [6]. Wang et al. (2012) also presented an improved version of the existing analytical model for EPB cutterhead torque and validated the parameters using real data from the Hangzhou Metro Line 1 [11].

Godinez et al. (2015) assessed cutterhead torque using a combination of analytical and measured field data and validated the method using monitoring data from the University Link (U-Link 230) metro tunnel project in Seattle, USA. The Japan Society of Civil Engineers developed a computational model to calculate the total thrust force required by the EPBM for excavation in soft urban soil [13].

For hard rock excavation, the necessary TBM cutterhead torque is determined by the torque of the cutting tools, and the overall thrust force is based on the required load to overcome the penetration force of the cutting tools [14,15]. Field data from a tunneling project in Iran was used to establish a new model for TBM performance prediction and project planning in hard and strong rocks [16 - 18]. Additionally, a TBM performance model was developed for soft sedimentary rocks based on data collected during the Zagros water conveyance tunnelling (ZWCT) project in Iran [19].

The geological characteristics that govern tunnel boring machine (TBM) performance are inherently unpredictable and deter­ ministic. To address this issue, soft computing techniques based on artificial intelligence (AI) have been developed as a means of predicting TBM performance. AI techniques offer a powerful method for implicitly detecting the complex nonlinear relationship be­ tween independent and dependent variables [8,20].

Several AI algorithms, including support vector machine (SVM), recurrent neural network (RNN), artificial neural network (ANN), fuzzy logic, and genetic algorithm (GA), have been utilized to estimate TBM performance and assess EPB operational parameters [21 - 23]. Yagiz &amp; Karahan (2011) have employed particle swarm optimization (PSO) and radial basis function network (RBF) to predict TBM penetration rate based on rock mass parameters while others have focused on real-time evaluation of TBM operational H. Samadi and J. Hassanpour parameters using RNN techniques [24]. Additionally, the multiple linear perceptron (ANN-MLP) method has been used to evaluate TBM advance rate with actual field data [25]. However, there has been a lack of attention to developing a predictor approach for assessing EPB operating parameters in semi-open rock conditions.

In this study, the existing theoretical model for EPB operational parameters in soft soil material has been improved to make it applicable for EPB tunneling in rock conditions. The overall cutting torque equation was modified to account for the use of disc cutters instead of rippers on the cutterhead. Additionally, the Takagi-Sugeno fuzzy system (TSFS) has been employed as a powerful predictor network to assess operating parameters, taking into consideration the best correlation between effective parameters and the target value. The proposed models were verified using monitoring data from the southern extension of Tehran metro line 6 (TML6-SE) in Iran, where predominantly limestone rock and sandy to clayey soil were encountered. The results of the computational model are in good agreement with the measured data.

## 3. Project description

The southern extension of Tehran ' s subway line 6 (TML6-SE) follows a route that traverse limestone rock and sandy to clayey soil. The project ' s geographical location is depicted in Fig. 1, while the geological profile along the tunnel is illustrated in Fig. 2. Tunnel excavation was carried out under an overburden ranging from 6 to 26 m. Exploration boreholes encountered two distinct material types, encompassing rock and soil, which included a combination of fine-grained and coarse-grained soil with varying consistencies. Notably, a monitoring cross-section has been established as the reference case, situated within a limestone rock setting characterized by uniaxial compressive strength (UCS) of 80 - 120 MPa and a rock mass fracturing degree (RQD) between 60 and 90. Geotechnical parameters pertaining to the reference section for TML6-SE are summarized in Table 1.

Excavating the 9.19 m tunnel in the Tehran metro line 6 project involved the use of a refurbished HERRENKNECHT EPBM (S-523), depicted in Fig. 3 (a, b). The TBM ' s cutterhead rotation is facilitated by 16 hydraulic motors. At a maximum rotation speed of 3.8 RPM, the machine exhibits torque and thrust values of 17197 kN m and 30000 KN, respectively. Instantaneous chamber pressure monitoring has been made possible through the installation of seven pressure sensors situated at the back of the chamber. The TBM cutterhead is equipped with 26 double cutting tools (43.18 m, 17 in) in the central and peripheral parts, resulting in a maximum radial opening rate of 28 %.

By under­ standing the torque and thrust forces involved, engineers can determine the appropriate machine specifications and parameters to successfully excavate tunnels while minimizing risks and ensuring safety. Additionally, this analysis helps in predicting the machine ' s performance, maintenance requirements, and overall project costs. Ultimately, considering cutterhead torque and thrust force is essential for achieving optimal results in tunnel construction projects.

## 5.2. The modified theoretical model (EPB in hard rock condition)

Shi et al. (2011) presented a theoretical model for a 1.8 m-diameter earth pressure balance (EPB) using laboratory simulators to study the behavior of soil materials. The study recommended eight components of cutterhead torque to consider the interaction be­ tween the cutterhead and tunnel face geo-materials. Among these components, the overall cutting torque (T4) and rotational bearing and sealing torque (T7 + T8) were found to be less significant. The majority of the cutterhead torque was attributed to the friction torque on the frontal, circular, and back surfaces (T1 + T2 + T3), the shearing torque on opening (T5), and the agitating torque (T6) [6]. The components of cutterhead torque is presented in Fig. 9(a - c).

The models developed by Shi et al., 2011 and JSCE 2007 were originally designed to calculate cutterhead torque and thrust force for soft soil materials using EPB machines. These models are commonly utilized in the field of soft ground tunneling where the cutting process mainly involves soft ground materials. Typically, the cutterhead of an EPB machine is equipped with multiple cutting tools arranged in a specific order. The total cutting torque generated during excavation is the cumulative torque exerted by each individual cutter.

As the EPB machine progresses forward, the cutters on the rotating cutterhead apply cutting forces in both axial and radial di­ rections onto the soil. These cutters encounter resistance forces from the soil stratum in the opposite direction. By neglecting the friction on the sides of the cutters, the cutting torque can be calculated using the formula provided by Shi et al., 2011.

## 7. Conclusions

The prediction of EPB performance is essential for the success and management of tunneling projects. This study proposes an improved analytical model and Takagi Sugeno Fuzzy System for predicting EPB cutterhead torque and thrust force in unstable rock conditions, taking into account influential operating parameters. These models are applied to actual measured data, providing an opportunity to enhance the utilization of EPB in rock materials. The suggested models are validated using a dataset of 457 data points from the Tehran metro line 6 southern extension sector. A modified analytical model is explored for estimating operating parameters and their impact on EPB performance in limestone rock conditions with specific RQD and UCS values. The results demonstrate a strong correlation between the obtained values and actual data. Based on the evaluation, slightly more than half of the overall cutterhead torque is attributed to the torque of the cutting tools, while the majority of the overall thrust force is expended on overcoming chamber pressure and the cutting discs penetrating the rock.

For developing and training soft computing techniques, it should be mention that the selection of input parameters for prediction in TBM tunneling using smart networks is a complex task in soft computing methods, requiring the use of correlation and statistical analysis to enhance predictive model accuracy. Penetration rate, earth pressure, thrust per cutter, and rotational speed of the cut­ terhead are chosen for predicting cutterhead torque, while penetration rate and earth pressure are selected for analyzing overall thrust force. After training the network and obtaining the its outcome, various evaluation metrics, including RRMSE, RMSE, RAE, MADE, MAPE, and MOE, are employed to assess the error of the predicted outputs using the TSFS model. The statistical indices indicate a commendable accuracy of the models. Furthermore, comparisons between theoretical calculations and TSFS methods are presented to illustrate the fuzzy model ' s capability in predicting EPB performance during excavation in unstable rock materials, while the use of a hybrid model is recommended to achieve reliable results.
